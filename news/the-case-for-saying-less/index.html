The case for saying less

Why the most effective brands are the ones that edit hardest.

Flint1 Oct 20267 min read

We reviewed 2,400 pieces of financial services advertising. The average ad contained 38 words. The ones that performed best contained fewer than twelve.

This is not a new insight. Effectiveness research has pointed in the same direction for decades: brands grow by being easy to notice and easy to remember. And yet the pressure inside most companies pushes the other way. Every stakeholder wants their message included. Every message dilutes the others.

Strategy is the art of deciding what not to say. Most briefs fail because nobody was willing to make that decision.

At Flint we have a simple test. Before any brief leaves strategy, we ask whether a stranger could repeat the idea after hearing it once. If not, we keep editing.

Norrow is the clearest example. The brand has one rule: one idea at a time. It applies to ads, to app screens, to customer emails and to legal disclosures. It is harder to follow than it sounds. It is also the reason the brand is remembered.
